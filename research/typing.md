# Typing — research notes

## 1. The short version
- **You don't need "proper" touch typing to be fast.** Self-taught typists can match trained ones. What fast people share: **the same finger always hits the same key**, hands stay still, and the next key is already on its way.
- **Fast typists overlap keys** (press the next before releasing the last). Slow typists almost never do. This comes from well-practised letter pairs, not from trying.
- **Accuracy and speed go together.** Every mistake costs the wrong key + backspace + the right key + losing your rhythm. Fewer fixes is the cheapest speed you can buy.
- **Eyes on the screen, not the keys.** Looking down is the habit that holds people back the longest.
- **Short and often beats long and rare.** About an hour a day beat two two-hour blocks in the classic keyboard study; for a quick app, a few focused minutes is the right shape.
- **Push a little past comfortable, briefly** — then back to normal. Comfort speed is where plateaus live.
- **Phone is a different skill.** Two thumbs is fastest; autocorrect helps; picking words from the suggestion bar slows most people down. PC training does not carry over to the phone.

## 2. What the evidence says

**PC (physical keyboard)**
- Feit et al. (2016) filmed 30 typists with motion capture: self-taught typists using fewer fingers reached speeds comparable to touch typists. Best predictors: consistent finger-to-key mapping, preparing upcoming keys, little hand movement. Their advice: touch typing is mostly worth it if you want to go past ~100 WPM consistently, and it takes several hundred hours.
- Dhakal et al. (2018), 168,000 people / 136M keystrokes: average 52 WPM. Trained typists were only ~5 WPM faster (small effect). Rollover correlated strongly with speed (r = 0.73): fast typists roll over on 40–70% of keys, slow ones almost never. Speed differences show up in **letter pairs typed by different fingers/hands**, not in key press length (about the same for everyone). Fewer corrections → faster (r = −0.36). Wrong-key (substitution) errors separate fast from slow typists most.
- Pinet et al. (2022), 1,301 students: today's young typists build real expertise mostly from everyday typing; the faster group typed more hours a day, but did not report more drill practice. Honest read: drills help when targeted; plain volume matters a lot.
- Baddeley & Longman (1978): keyboard learning was most efficient in 1 h/day sessions, least in 2×2 h/day.
- Yechiam et al. (2003): after a touch-typing course, people drift back to looking at keys because it feels faster right now. Making "eyes on screen" pay off right away kept the new skill.
- "Type slightly faster than comfortable" (Ericsson's deliberate practice, retold in Foer's *Moonwalking with Einstein*): **some** evidence, mostly secondhand.

**Phone (touchscreen)**
- Palin et al. (2019), 37,000 people: average 36 WPM, ~15 WPM slower than PC; two thumbs fastest (38 WPM avg). Autocorrect users were fastest (43 WPM); heavy word-prediction use correlated with slower typing. A PC touch-typing course had **no** link to phone speed. Errors cost more on phones (looking up to check, awkward fixing), so people leave more uncorrected.

**Is full touch typing worth it for Leo?** Probably not as a rebuild. Switching drops your speed for weeks. The bigger win per minute: keep his own style but make it **consistent** (same finger per key), stop looking down, and clean up his slowest letter pairs.

## 3. Practice modes to build

| Mode | On screen | Struggle | Growth shows as | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Weak pairs drill** | Short real words loaded with his 3–5 slowest/most-missed bigrams (from per-key data), 30–60 s | The few pairs that drag the average | Those pairs' time drops; list changes | Strong (bigram timing predicts speed) |
| **Clean run** | Words keep coming only while correct; first error ends the run; shows length of clean streak in the run (not saved as a streak) | Rushing, backspace cost | Longer clean runs, fewer corrections in the main test | Strong |
| **Pace line** | A moving cursor at test speed +10%, 30 s, then one run at normal speed | Plateau at comfort speed | Normal WPM creeps up | Some |
| **Eyes up** | Words appear and the typed text is hidden; occasional symbol flashes on screen to tap/call | Looking at keys | Accuracy holds without looking | Some (Yechiam) |
| **Rhythm / listen** | Metronome click; type one letter per beat on common words, tempo rises slowly; can also hear the word spoken instead of reading it | Uneven, stop-start typing; uses his ear | Steadier gaps between keys | Weak–some |
| **Thumbs (phone only)** | Real sentences, autocorrect on vs off as a choice, shows corrections cost | Phone error-fixing | Fewer backspaces per word | Some |

**Build first:** (1) **Weak pairs drill** — uses data he already records, biggest gain per minute, and it's *his* list. (2) **Clean run** — attacks the most expensive habit (errors) and teaches accuracy-first without lectures. (3) **Rhythm / listen** — fits how Leo learns best (hearing and doing) and is fun; evidence is lighter, so treat it as a try.

## 4. Methods and principles

**Methods (tips to carry into any practice)**
- Eyes on the screen; if you peek, notice it — no fixing it mid-word.
- Same finger for the same key, every time, even if it's not the "official" finger.
- Hands stay put, fingers move.
- Slow down until mistakes nearly stop, then speed up.
- Read the next word while typing this one.
- Phone: two thumbs, let autocorrect work, skip the suggestion bar.

**Principles (built into the test and every mode)**
- Show accuracy next to WPM, always; show corrections (backspaces), not just final errors.
- Track PC and phone separately — never compare across.
- Short sessions (30 s–3 min); suggest stopping after a few runs.
- Compare him with his own past (median of last runs), never with others; no streaks, points or prizes.
- Drills use real common words, not nonsense strings.
- Feedback names a concrete thing ("'th' got 30 ms faster"), not "great job".

## 5. What changes a score that isn't skill
- **Keyboard:** laptop vs mechanical, layout, key travel; some cheap keyboards limit rollover.
- **Phone:** autocorrect on/off, keyboard app, screen size, one hand vs two, walking.
- **Warm-up:** the first run is usually slower — maybe don't count it.
- **Word list:** short common words inflate WPM; punctuation, capitals and numbers drop it. Keep the list fixed when comparing.
- **Test length:** 15 s scores run higher than 60 s.
- **"Good at the test" vs real typing:** the test has no thinking, no punctuation, no editing. Real typing is composing — slower. Getting good at the test is fine; just don't confuse the two.
